Claude Code for Insurance Agents

Your Complete AI Setup Guide

By the end of this, Claude will know your business, understand how you think and talk, connect to your tools, and run your sales pipeline — all from one conversation at a time.

Read This First

Claude Code is different from a regular AI chat. It runs on your computer, reads and writes files, connects to your tools, and builds systems for you. You don't need to code. You talk to it like a smart assistant — it builds.

Quick Win — Windows Voice Typing

Press Win + H anywhere on your computer — including inside Claude — to dictate by voice. Use this constantly. Most of what you'll do with Claude is faster spoken than typed.

Feeling Overwhelmed? Start Here

Don't try to do this all at once. Just do these three things first:

  1. Install Claude Code (Step 1 — one terminal command)
  2. Build your Business Profile (Step 2 — Claude interviews you)
  3. Build your Personal Profile (Step 3 — teaches Claude your voice)

Everything else can wait. Those three steps alone will make Claude 10x more useful than any generic AI chat.

⚠ Important — Medicare Compliance

Insurance — especially Medicare — is one of the most regulated industries in the country. Claude will write whatever you ask it to write. You are still legally responsible for everything you send or publish.

CMS (the federal agency that oversees Medicare) has strict rules about marketing language, required disclaimers, prohibited claims, and outreach methods. Violations can cost you your license.

Before posting or sending any Medicare-related content Claude helped write, run this check:

Review this content for CMS Medicare marketing compliance. Flag anything that could violate CMS guidelines — restricted language, missing disclaimers, prohibited claims, anything that implies guaranteed benefits. I'm responsible for final approval, but flag every concern you see. [paste your content here]

Also feed your state's Medicare marketing guidelines into your knowledge vault early — Claude will apply them automatically once they're in there. When in doubt, run it by your FMO compliance team before it goes out.

Part 1
Set Up Claude's Brain

Before you connect a single tool, do these steps. They're what make Claude feel like it already knows you — because it will.

Step 1

Install Claude Code

One terminal window. After this, you live in the Claude desktop app.

1. Install Node.js

Go to nodejs.org, click the green Download button (LTS version), run the installer, click through the defaults.

2. Open Windows Terminal

Press the Windows key, type Terminal or PowerShell, open it.

3. Install Claude Code

npm install -g @anthropic-ai/claude-code

4. Launch it

claude

Sign in with your Anthropic account. You live here from now on.

If anything breaks: Copy the error message, paste it into Claude, ask what to do. It'll walk you through the fix. This is the pattern for everything.

Step 2

Build Your Business Profile

This is the most important step. Your Business Profile is the file Claude reads at the start of every single conversation. It's your briefing document — who you are, what you sell, who you serve, how your practice works. The richer this is, the more useful Claude becomes on every email, client call, and strategy question.

Claude will interview you and build it. You just answer honestly.

Paste this into Claude to start
I want to build a complete business profile so you understand my insurance practice inside and out. This will become a file you read at the start of every conversation. Interview me thoroughly. Ask me about: - My name, business name, and location - Every product line I sell (Medicare Advantage, private health, business insurance, hospital indemnity, life insurance, and anything else) - Who my ideal clients are — seniors turning 65, families, small business owners, referral sources - How my sales process works from first contact to enrollment - My FMO or parent agency relationship and what they provide - Where I am in the business right now (just launched, no clients yet, building pipeline) - What tools I use: Gmail, Google Calendar, Calendly, Zoom, Todoist, Facebook, Instagram - What I struggle with most and what I want Claude to help me with - How I want Claude to talk to me — casual, direct, like a business partner After the interview, build the file, save it as CLAUDE.md, and confirm it's saved. Then tell me: what would make this profile even more useful over time?
What your Business Profile should cover when done:
  • Your name, business name, and location
  • Every product line with a one-line description of each
  • Your ideal client types, ranked by priority
  • Your full sales pipeline stages (lead → quote → application → enrolled → renewal)
  • Your FMO name and what support they provide
  • Every tool you use and what you use it for
  • Your current business stage and biggest priorities
  • Decision rules: always ask before sending emails or taking external actions

Iterate, don't perfect. Anytime Claude gets something wrong or you learn something new about your business, say: "Update my CLAUDE.md to reflect this." It gets sharper every week.

Step 3

Build Your Personal Profile

This is what most people skip — and it's what separates a generic AI assistant from one that actually sounds like you. Your Personal Profile teaches Claude your voice, your personality, how you communicate, what you care about, and what kind of thinking partner you want it to be.

When Claude writes an email for you, it should sound like you wrote it. When Claude gives you advice, it should talk to you the way a trusted colleague would. That only happens when Claude knows you — not just your business.

Paste this into Claude to start
I want to build a personal profile so you understand who I am, how I think, and how I communicate. This will help you write in my voice, give me advice that fits how I make decisions, and show up the way I actually need you to — not in a generic way. Interview me on all of this: MY COMMUNICATION STYLE - How do I naturally talk? Formal or casual? Direct or conversational? - What words or phrases do I use a lot? - What do I hate reading? (buzzwords, corporate speak, anything fake or fluffy) - How long should your answers be — short and punchy, or thorough and detailed? MY PERSONALITY & BACKGROUND - What's my background before insurance? How did I get here? - Why did I get into this business specifically? What's the real reason? - What do I care most about in my work — helping people, building something, the income, the freedom? - What are my strengths? What do I know I'm not as strong at? HOW I WANT YOU TO SHOW UP - Do I want you to push back when I have a bad idea, or just help me execute? - Do I want advice and opinions, or just help completing tasks? - What would make you annoying to work with? What would make you invaluable? - Is there anything you should never say or do? MY GOALS - Where do I want this business to be in 1 year? 3 years? - What does success look like for me personally — not just in business? - What's the thing I most want Claude to help me build or figure out? After the interview, save everything as a personal profile in my knowledge vault. Then write me a short "how to write like Trish" style guide — the rules Claude should follow every time it writes something in my voice.
What your Personal Profile should capture when done:
  • Your communication style — casual vs. formal, short vs. detailed
  • Words and phrases you use naturally (and ones that sound fake on you)
  • Your background story and why you're in insurance
  • How you want Claude to challenge you vs. support you
  • Your 1-year and 3-year vision
  • A "write like Trish" style guide Claude follows for every email and post

After this is built, test it. Ask Claude to draft a cold outreach message to a senior turning 65. If it doesn't sound like you, say: "That doesn't sound like me. Here's how I would actually say it: [your version]. Update my style guide."

Step 4

Build Your Knowledge Vault

Your vault is Claude's long-term memory. It's a folder of plain text files on your computer — carrier summaries, product comparisons, scripts, client notes, compliance rules. The richer it gets, the sharper Claude becomes on every answer it gives you.

Install Obsidian (free)

Go to obsidian.md, download the Windows version, install it. Obsidian shows your vault as a browsable folder — Claude reads and writes the same files.

Paste this into Claude to set up the vault
I want to set up an Obsidian vault as my long-term knowledge base for my insurance practice. First, propose a folder structure that fits an insurance agent — I need folders for things like: carriers, products, client notes, leads, scripts, objection handlers, compliance rules, SOPs, and marketing content. Create the folder structure on my computer. Then tell me how to open the vault in Obsidian. After that, I want to start filling it. I'll hand you files and documents — carrier product sheets, FMO training materials, scripts, any notes I have. Read each one, ask me clarifying questions about anything worth capturing as a permanent rule, and save what's useful into the right place in the vault.

What to feed it first

  • Carrier product sheets and benefit summaries
  • FMO onboarding and training materials
  • Any scripts or objection handlers you've been given
  • Medicare guidelines for your state
  • Your personal "why I do this" story (Claude will write this with you)

The compounding effect: After every client call, every training, every product update — add it to the vault. After 90 days Claude will know your product lines better than most people you'd hire.

Part 2
Connect Your Tools

Once Claude knows you and your business, connect the tools you already use. This is when it goes from smart assistant to operating system.

Step 5

Connect Google Workspace

This is the biggest unlock. Gmail, Google Calendar, Google Sheets, and Google Docs all connect at once. Claude can read your inbox, draft replies in your voice, manage your calendar, and run your entire pipeline tracker — without you leaving the app.

Paste this into Claude
I want to connect my Google Workspace account so you can read my Gmail, draft and send emails on my behalf, manage my Google Calendar, and read and edit my Google Sheets and Docs. Walk me through setup step by step. I'm not technical — explain what I'm clicking and why. Make sure we publish the OAuth app to Production status so the connection doesn't expire every 7 days.

Don't skip Production status. New connections default to "Testing" mode and break every 7 days. Publishing to Production takes 2 minutes and means you never have to reconnect.

Test it

Show me my last 5 emails. What's the most recent one I haven't replied to?

If that works, you're connected.

Step 6

Connect Your Other Tools

Calendly

When someone books a call, Claude sees it and can draft confirmation and follow-up messages automatically. Go to calendly.com if you don't have an account yet.

I use Calendly for scheduling. I want to connect it so you can see my bookings and help me send confirmation and follow-up messages to anyone who books a call. Walk me through setting up the Calendly MCP connector.

Zoom

Claude can see your upcoming meetings, help you prep for calls, and draft follow-ups after. Get Zoom at zoom.us.

I use Zoom for client and prospect calls. Connect it so you can see my scheduled meetings, help me prep, and draft follow-up messages after calls. Walk me through the Zoom MCP connector setup.

Todoist — Your Task Manager

You don't have a task manager yet. Add Todoist now — it's free, works great on Windows and Android, and connects directly to Claude. Sign up at todoist.com, then install the Windows app and Android app.

I just set up Todoist as my task manager. Connect it so you can add tasks, check what's due, and help me stay on top of follow-ups. My API token is in Todoist under Settings → Integrations → Developer. Walk me through the Todoist MCP connector setup.

Your FMO CRM

Before you decide whether to use your FMO's CRM or build your own pipeline, find out what it can actually do.

My FMO gave me access to a CRM called [name of CRM]. I haven't set it up yet. Look it up and tell me: does it have an API or MCP connector? Can you connect to it? If not, should I use it alongside a Google Sheet pipeline or instead of it? Give me a real recommendation.

Facebook & Instagram

No direct connector — but Claude writes every post, you copy and publish. Manage your posts at Meta Business Suite (free scheduling tool for both platforms).

I want to post consistently on Facebook and Instagram to build my audience. I'm targeting seniors and families in [your city/area]. Help me build a 30-day content calendar. Write each caption in my voice — conversational, helpful, not salesy. I'll copy and paste to publish.

Batch it. One morning per week, 30 minutes with Claude, 4–5 posts written. Schedule them in Meta Business Suite and forget it.

Part 3
Build Your Systems

This is where setup becomes leverage. Two systems that run your sales pipeline and protect your renewal income.

Step 7

Build Your AI Sales Engine

One Google Sheet holds every prospect. Every morning Claude emails you a prioritized list with follow-up messages already drafted in your voice — ready to send. After every call, you tell Claude what happened in one sentence and it handles the rest.

Paste this into Claude — let it interview you first
I want to build an AI sales engine for my insurance practice. Before you build anything, interview me: - How am I tracking contacts today? - What falls through the cracks most often? - What does my pipeline look like — what are the stages from first contact to enrolled client? - Who are my main contact types? (Medicare prospects, families, small business owners, referral sources like doctors and financial advisors) After the interview, build three things: 1. A Google Sheet — one row per contact. Columns: name, type (Medicare prospect / family / small business / referral source), product interest, stage, last touch date, next touch date, warmth score 1–5, notes. 2. A daily brief — runs each morning, reads the sheet, and emails me a prioritized list of who to contact today. For each contact, draft a follow-up message in my voice, ready to send from Gmail. 3. A logging prompt — after any call or meeting, I describe what happened in plain English. You update the sheet, log the notes, and queue the next follow-up in Todoist. Start with the interview. Then build step by step.

Why this works when other systems don't: Most CRMs require you to log in, remember to check, and write your own outreach. This system pushes to you. You wake up and the brief is already in your inbox with everything written.

Step 8

Build Your Policy & Renewal Tracker

Renewals are your second income stream — and most new agents lose clients at year one simply because nobody followed up. Build this now, before you have clients, so the habit is already there when you do.

I want to build a policy and renewal tracker in Google Sheets. Columns I need: client name, product type, carrier, effective date, renewal date, annual premium, my estimated commission, current status, and notes. After building the sheet: 1. Set up a monthly email alert listing every client whose renewal is within 60 days 2. Write me a renewal check-in call script — what to say, what to ask, how to make it feel personal not transactional 3. Add a "commission received" column so I can track actual income until I'm ready to move to a bookkeeping tool

On accounting: When you're ready, QuickBooks connects directly to Claude. For now, your renewal tracker doubles as your income log.

Step 9

Build Your Carrier Appointment Tracker

As a new agent you'll get appointed with multiple carriers across multiple product lines. That's a lot to track — which carriers, which products, which states, appointment status, effective dates, and renewal windows. Most agents keep this in their head or scattered across emails. Don't. Build the tracker now while you're getting appointed, so it's clean from day one.

Paste this into Claude
I want to build a carrier appointment tracker in Google Sheets. I get appointed with multiple carriers across multiple product lines — Medicare Advantage, private health, business insurance, hospital indemnity, and life insurance. I may be appointed in multiple states. Build a sheet that tracks: - Carrier name - Product line (Medicare Advantage / Private Health / Business / Hospital Indemnity / Life) - State(s) I'm appointed in - Appointment status (pending / active / inactive) - Effective date - Renewal / re-appointment date - FMO or direct appointment - Contracting contact name and email - Notes Pre-fill it with the carriers I tell you about. Then set up a reminder that emails me 60 days before any appointment renewal date so I never lapse accidentally.

Also feed this into your vault. Once the tracker is built, ask Claude to save a summary of your carrier lineup to your knowledge vault — so when a prospect asks "do you work with [carrier]?" Claude can answer instantly from your own data.

The Real Skill

Just Tell Claude What You Want

Everything above is setup. The most valuable thing you'll learn from this guide is the pattern you'll use for everything that comes after it.

  1. Notice the friction. You don't need to know what's possible. Just notice what's costing you time or attention. "I keep forgetting to follow up." "I don't know what to say when someone pushes back on price." "I don't know which referral sources are actually working."
  2. Get roughly clear on what you want instead. Not perfect. Rough. "I want a follow-up sequence." "I want a price objection script." "I want a monthly referral report."
  3. Ask Claude to plan it first, not build it. Say: "Before you build anything, tell me what you'd build, what it would use, and what you'd need from me." Read the plan. Push back on what doesn't make sense. Then say go.
  4. Iterate. First version won't be perfect. Use it, tell Claude what's off, fix it. Three passes and you'll have something better than most off-the-shelf tools.
  5. Save what works. Ask Claude: "Save this as a skill so I can run it again with one command."
